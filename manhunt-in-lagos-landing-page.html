The Story

Welcome to Lagos.
Now try to survive it.

She flew in for anonymity — a quiet place to disappear after a relationship that ended badly. What she finds instead is a city that never stays quiet for long: the heat, the horns, the crowds that can turn in an instant.

When a protest boils over and thugs move in on the chaos, a stranger pulls her out of it. He knows these streets — not as a place of opportunity, but as a battlefield of power, loyalty, and things best left unspoken.

One rescue. One chain reaction. And suddenly, they're the ones being hunted.

Enforcers want payback. Officers who should protect them start closing ranks instead. Every checkpoint is a risk. Every safe house stops being safe. Lagos in Manhunt in Lagos isn't the enemy — it's the arena: vast, alive, and indifferent to who makes it out.

MANHUNT IN LAGOS
Chapter One
AN UNEXPECTED ENCOUNTER

On a warm afternoon in Lagos State, Nigeria, the sun cast its radiant glow, enveloping everything in its golden embrace. Amidst this vibrant backdrop, a striking figure emerged at the entrance of Murtala Muhammed International Airport. Clad in a pink skirt and black shirt, a curly-haired woman with a fair complexion navigated her way through the bustling surroundings.

Despite her elegance, she couldn't hide her discomfort under the relentless sun, occasionally hissing in annoyance as she wrestled with her cumbersome holdall bag and handbag. Each step seemed heavier than the last, reminiscent of the weight she felt during her departure from the United States.

Approaching the entrance, she exchanged a nod with a security guard, though inwardly lamenting the lack of assistance offered. Shouldn't they be more attentive to the needs of travellers? Instead, they appeared preoccupied with trivial matters, neglecting their duty to safeguard those entering and exiting the airport.

Unbeknownst to her, fate had cast her on a perilous path. Unravelling events would soon reveal the impending danger lurking in the shadows, threatening to ensnare her unsuspecting soul.

“Great, now what?” she muttered to herself, stepping out of the gate and finding no transportation in sight. Frustration boiled within her, prompting her to stomp her foot before dropping her bag. With determination, she retrieved a stylish white sun hat adorned with a vibrant red band from her bag, slipping it on to shield herself from the blazing sun. Retrieving her iPhone XS Max from her handbag, she swiftly launched Google, hoping to summon an Uber.

Suddenly, a deep voice interrupted her thoughts. “Hello there! Need a ride?” she heard from behind. Looking up, she met the gaze of a dark-skinned man with intricate tribal markings adorning his face, seated confidently behind the wheel of a well-worn 2006 Toyota Corolla, painted in striking yellow with dark stripes accentuating its frame.

Relief washed over her as she rummaged through her bag, causing the driver to watch her with curiosity. Moments later, she produced a folded sheet of paper, which she handed to him after smoothing it out. Nodding in understanding, the driver chuckled lightly. “I know the way,” he assured her, prompting a smile of gratitude as she eagerly settled into the backseat, ready to embark on her journey.

Lost in contemplation, the outsider in this unfamiliar land observed her surroundings with a wearied gaze. Amidst the chaotic scene, she couldn't help but notice a multitude of injustices unfolding before her eyes.

Firstly, the reckless driving of the vehicles, especially the commercial ones, caught her attention. They careened through the streets without regard for safety, with conductors hanging precariously from the bus doors, hurling insults at anyone in their path, particularly fellow drivers.

Then, there were the hawkers, scattered along the roadside in a seemingly haphazard manner. Their bold displays of goods and casual demeanour astonished her. Some even ventured onto the main road, exacerbating the already congested traffic. She couldn't help but wonder if they valued their own lives, considering the potential dangers of their actions.

As the car's pace slowed to a crawl, she leaned forward to investigate the cause. Ahead, a sea of protesters brandished placards and banners, their chants ringing out with an air of defiance. Their discontent was palpable, adding another layer of complexity to the bustling streetscape she was trying to navigate. Adjusting herself in her seat, she continued to observe, her gaze fixed on the unfolding scene outside the confines of the moving vehicle.

“What's happening?” she asked anxiously.

“Xenophobia, Sister! Xenophobia…” the driver replied calmly, his tribal markings accentuating his solemn expression.

“Xenophobia?” she echoed, puzzled.

“Yes, xenophobia,” he repeated slowly, sensing her confusion. “South Africa is targeting our people there,” he explained softly.

Realisation dawned on her. “Oh…” she murmured, placing a hand to her forehead before sinking back into her seat. “That's xenophobia,” she declared, now understanding.

The driver glanced at her through the rear-view mirror, impressed by her comprehension. “Exactly. They're targeting Nigerians with xenophobic attacks,” he confirmed, a hint of pride in his voice.

“Why?” she inquired.

With a heavy sigh, he explained, “They accuse us of being responsible for their problems. They label us as drug dealers and fraudsters, blaming us for their troubles. They claim it's 'South Africa for South Africans first'...”

Anger simmered beneath his words, reflecting the frustration of his people. His passenger could only respond with a somber grunt, grappling with the complexity of the situation. Aware of the truth behind the stereotypes, she couldn't ignore the impact of the actions of a few on the perception of the entire Nigerian community.

However, despite her initial enthusiasm, it was her chosen destination after her parents granted her the opportunity to spend a year in any country of her choosing. Though she couldn't pinpoint the exact reason for her decision, she was drawn to Nigeria's vast land, vibrant culture, and renowned hospitality, particularly that of the Yoruba people in the western region.

Yet, amidst her expectations, she found herself grappling with unexpected disappointments. It seemed that a temporary escape was the only remedy for her disillusionment.

Suddenly, the driver's impassioned outburst snapped her back to reality. “It's not their fault,” he exclaimed, his frustration palpable. “They celebrate because our leader lacks sensitivity,” he continued, referring to the president.

“Your president?” she inquired, intrigued.

“Yes, him. He's been silent since these troubles began. He hides in Aso Rock, too weak to lead,” the driver lamented, his hands clenched with anger.

“That's unfortunate,” she sympathised.

“It's worse than you imagine. Our former leader, General Abacha, would have taken swift action. But this one…” he trailed off, his disdain evident.

“Is he that ineffective?” she queried.

“Absolutely,” the driver affirmed, his voice tinged with bitterness. “We face countless challenges every day, with little hope for improvement. Many of us seek a better life elsewhere, desperate to escape the hardships. But where are the opportunities?” he concluded, his words heavy with resignation.

As they lapsed into silence, she pondered his words, her gaze drifting to the buildings lining the streets. Despite the hardships, there was resilience in the cityscape, a testament to the spirit of its inhabitants.

“What brings you to our city?” the driver inquired, eyeing her through the rearview mirror.

“I'm here for a holiday,” she replied softly. “But I'm starting to doubt if I'll enjoy any of it,” she added with a hint of coldness.

“Lagos?” he exclaimed incredulously, his laughter filling the cab. Unfazed by her stoic demeanour, he continued, “Lagos is the jewel of Nigeria, perhaps even Africa. You'll love it here. Many of your fellow countrymen do. They've made it their home for decades.”

“Can you be my go-to driver while I'm here?” she asked, cutting through his enthusiasm.

“Absolutely! Driving is my livelihood. I'll give you my number so you can call me whenever you need a ride,” he replied eagerly, watching her retrieve her phone from her bag. After dictating his number, he suggested, “Just save it as 'Taxi Man' for convenience.”

“Done,” she confirmed with a chuckle, impressed by his pragmatism.

Suddenly, they encountered a disturbance ahead. It was clear that trouble was brewing as those involved were scrutinising passing vehicles, even signalling those with tinted windows to roll them down or face consequences.

“What's happening? They don't seem friendly,” she observed, her heart racing as they approached the commotion.

Without answering, the driver swiftly shifted into reverse, attempting to retreat from the brewing chaos. However, his efforts were in vain as their vehicle was targeted by another group from behind, smashing their rear taillight in anger.

Bringing the car to a halt, the driver glanced apprehensively at the chaos unfolding behind them through the rearview mirror. His passenger, now beyond agitation, began to panic as the situation escalated.

With aggression, one of the menacing hoodlums peered into the car through the left-side window, spotting the occupants and shouting in Yorùbá, “This is one of them!” He forcefully opened the door and dragged out the white woman. In a matter of moments, the entire gang converged, flinging her luggage to the ground and snatching her handbag. They hurled accusations in a language she couldn't comprehend, accusing her of being complicit in the violence against their people.

The driver, feeble in his attempt to intervene, was met with a barrage of blows that left him weak and swollen-faced. Amidst the chaos, the woman was pushed and shoved violently, watched by a crowd of onlookers who offered no assistance, only jeers.

Suddenly, a young man dressed in a distinctive outfit emerged from the crowd, his presence commanding respect. With murmurs of recognition and reverence, the crowd parted to let him through. Just as the assailants prepared to deliver a final blow, he intervened, saving her from a potentially fatal fall.

“You're not South African, are you?” he demanded, his voice hoarse with emotion.

Shaken but standing, the woman met his gaze, only to find herself facing a handsome, fair-skinned man. “I'm American,” she revealed, her voice trembling.

Realization dawned on the man, and he crouched to retrieve her hat, now dirtied and disheveled. “I'm deeply sorry,” he offered, dusting off the hat before returning it to her. “You were mistaken for a South African. Surely you've heard about the atrocities against our people there?”

“And does that justify treating me like this?” she erupted, her voice laced with indignation. “I've been humiliated beyond belief. This is not how you express your grief. It's madness,” she concluded, her words hanging heavily in the air as the man stood before her, unmoved.

The atmosphere erupted with furious jeers as she uttered her final words. Terrifying threats reverberated through the crowd, some shouted in Yorùbá with traces of English, demanding fuel and a tire. “Elọ mu epo bẹntiroolu wa nibi… Go and bring some fuel here. Tyre da? Se o ya were… Are you mad?”

Panic seized her as she heard the ominous words. “I heard them mention fuel and a tire. What do they plan to do?” she asked anxiously, turning to the young man beside her. She recoiled slightly, as if anticipating danger from the objects mentioned.

“I believe…” he began calmly. “I believe they're preparing to set you on fire,” he disclosed evenly, meeting her gaze with unwavering resolve. She froze in terror.
